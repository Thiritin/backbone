# Introduction

Source text for the in-game introduction, opened from "First time here?" on the start screen and from the Backbone menu. Each `##` heading is one page.

## Welcome

Congratulations. You have just founded yet another hosting company, in a market that already has far too many of them.

It is January 2005. You have €5,000, one server in your basement, a home internet line and an inbox. Your goal is to keep your customers happy, grow from that basement into datacenters across Europe and, one day, into a datacenter of your own, while handling the dull side of running a business.

Your customers will not pay their invoices on time. Your software is pretty dumb. Nobody said running a business would be fun.

## Pear One

To run all of this you bought into **Pear One**, the all-in-one hosting platform from Pear. It looks suspiciously like a computer from a certain fruit company, and it promises to handle everything from racks to invoices.

The base is free. You already paid for the hardware. And you can easily extend your experience with ~~DLCs~~ **feature expansions** from the **Pear Store** once you have learned enough to use them. You start with a handful of apps and do almost everything by hand, from your inbox.

Of course Pear would never abuse the fact that this is a subscription service by charging you per customer. No company would ever come to that conclusion.\*

Thanks to the EU and its rules on fair competition, Pear has to offer competing products. So some things come in several "flavours", such as the virtualization platform that runs your virtual machines or the software that monitors your servers. Each has its upsides and downsides, and switching later hurts.

\*Pear One feature expansions include monthly subscriptions, per-device, per-customer and per-employee pricing, a percentage of your revenue, yearly price adjustments and dynamic in-app purchases.

## Automate, then regret it

As you grow, you automate your work, which mostly replaces old headaches with new ones. You can hire people, or spend your whole salary on AI tokens and hope the bot fixes it.

The bot deleted a customer's disk. Well, that is not your problem. Except it is.

You will also argue with the bank about your loan. If you love accounting, this is the game for you.

## Words you will meet

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Region | A city where you have infrastructure, such as Frankfurt (fra) |
| Availability zone | One datacenter location inside a region, such as fra1 or fra2. Racks, storage, networks and customer bookings belong to exactly one zone |
| Position | One rack's worth of floor space in a datacenter |
| Rack share | Part of a rack the datacenter owns: 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 or a full rack |
| Cage, suite | A locked area, or a whole room, for many of your own racks |
| U | One rack unit, 4.45 cm of rack height. A 1U server is the flattest kind |
| Carrier panel | Where the datacenter hands you the internet |
| Transit, peering, exchange | Paying a carrier to reach the internet, swapping traffic with other networks for free, and the place where networks meet to do that |
| Cluster | A group of your servers that runs customer workloads together |
| Workload | Something a customer runs on your servers: a virtual machine, a web space, a game server |
| Overcommit | Selling more than you physically have, because customers rarely use everything at once |
| RAID | Spreading data over several drives so one failed drive loses nothing |
| SLA | The uptime you promise a customer. Break it and they want money back |
| MRR | Monthly recurring revenue: what your customers pay you every month |
| Dunning | Politely, then less politely, asking customers to pay |
| Lease | Paying for hardware in monthly instalments instead of all at once |
