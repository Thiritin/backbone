# Staff and AI bots

Staff are named people with a skill level, a work rate and a salary. Juniors cost little and grow into experts; seniors are good from day one but cost far more, as on the real job market.

**Employees.** Each employee has a name, role, skill (0–100), work rate, salary and tenure. Work rate rises with skill. Skill grows with tenure, fastest in the first years. The salary is set by skill at hiring, and yearly raises stay small, so a junior who stays is the cheapest expert you can get. Once a year each employee asks for a raise, and refusing makes them more likely to leave and take their skill with them.

**Training.** A new employee knows nothing about your processes. Before they handle a ticket type or a task, they are trained on it, which takes time and money, and each skill is trained separately: an agent trained on billing questions still cannot handle abuse reports. Once an employee is fully trained, you can make them a trainer. A trainer does nothing but train others until nobody is left to train, then goes back to their normal work. Once you have staff, they handle the work across all of these areas, including abuse and legal requests.

**Technicians.** A technician works at one location, the basement when hired, and works through that location's queue of operations in office hours, one operation per hour. An operation is one job on one device: replacing a failed or worn part with a spare from the zone's storage, shutting a device down or starting it, draining a cluster node (moving all its VMs off counts as a single operation however many move), or upgrading one node of a cluster to the current PHP version. Operations on the same device run in queue order, and an operation waits while a swap is under way or no spare is in storage. A part that only swaps with the server off costs three operations: the technician shuts the server down, swaps the part and starts it again. A technician's swap takes 15 minutes and bills no remote hands. Technicians train on three kinds of operation like support agents train on ticket types: parts and power, draining servers, and PHP upgrades.

The Locations inspector shows a button next to Replace, Shut down or Power on, and Drain that queues the operation and names the technician and the hour, such as "Lena, 10:00"; a queued operation shows Queued with a Cancel. The cluster's PHP row in the inspector upgrades every node by hand or queues one upgrade per node. Without a technician the player keeps doing everything directly: swaps in the basement are free and immediate, at a datacenter the provider's remote hands do them as before.

**Repair rules.** Technicians follow two rules without the player, set in the Staff app on any technician: Replace failed parts (on by default) queues a swap for each failed part a spare in storage can cover, and Replace worn parts (off by default) does the same for parts past their life, only for parts that swap while the device runs, so the rule never takes customers offline. The rules work at every location with a technician trained on parts and power. A rule to keep a minimum of spares in storage is not built yet.

**On-call duty.** In the Staff app the player puts technicians trained on parts and power into the on-call rotation of their location. Duty rotates weekly through the rotation in the order technicians joined it, with the handover on Monday at 09:00; a single technician in the rotation is always on call. Outside office hours the on-call technician comes in for urgent work only, one thing per call-out hour, in this order: a DDoS attack that saturates the location's links gets blackholed (the third rule, Blackhole attacks, on by default), then urgent operations from the location's queue run (a swap of a failed part, which the failed-parts rule queues, and starting a device again, since a device left off is an outage), then up to two open abuse reports get the right reply. Abuse reports come by email and belong to no location, so the first on-call technician without other work answers them. Worn parts, draining, PHP upgrades and shutdowns wait for office hours. A technician called out needs 11 hours of rest after the last call-out hour and starts late the next day, so a rotation of several technicians keeps the office hours covered. The Locations inspector names the on-call technician when an urgent job would go to them, such as "Leon (on call), Sat 02:00". The pay follows the German public-sector model and is paid with the monthly payroll as two lines per technician: an allowance per on-call day and the call-out hours. Numbers are in economy/on-call-duty.md, On-call duty.

**PHP versions.** Every cluster node runs a PHP version from a dated catalogue, 4.4 in 2005 and then each release from 5.1 to 8.5 from the year after it came out. A new node gets the current version; a cluster's sites run on its oldest node's version. Old PHP raises how-to tickets a little, a version three or more releases behind is unsupported and brings more abuse reports from hacked sites, and from three releases behind some web hosting requests in Sales need a newer version and go to another host. Upgrading a node slows its sites for an hour. Numbers are in economy/php-versions.md, PHP versions.

**Security.** A few times in the game's history PHP has a serious vulnerability (economy/security-advisories.md, Security advisories). It only reaches a player who runs web sites on an affected version, and it unfolds over two and a half game days: news outlets and the PHP project announce it with a patch date, someone on Twister posts a proof of concept, the Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der Informationstechnick warns to take affected systems offline, and the patch comes out on the announced date. Each stage pauses the game and opens a sheet like the first-time setup, with a button to the Security app. The Security app lists the affected nodes with their cluster, PHP version, sites, and whether they are online, offline, patched or compromised, with Take offline, Bring online, Patch and Clean up. From the proof of concept on, every unpatched node with its sites online can be compromised, more often after the agency warning: its sites are defaced and down, its customers get a data leak notice, send abuse reports and write bad reviews, and the node stays down until the player cleans it up from backups or rebuilds it. Offline sites count as outages, so the player weighs hours of planned downtime against the chance of a breach. Patching is the PHP upgrade, by hand or queued for a technician, and only works once the patch is out. A compromised host also sends spam: every day it stays compromised its mail address collects spam like any other source and loses reputation until it is listed, and its web sites run slower at 70% performance, so mail customers there write in about bounces until the player cleans the host up. **Customer applications.** Every web hosting service runs an app: WordPass, Joomlah, Drupel or plain HTML, drawn from the market shares of its start year. Each app has vulnerabilities of its own every year, like the company's software, and each site records which release it runs and when it was last updated. After a patch comes out, most customers update by themselves within a week, the more likely the more skilled they are, and WordPass sites do so far more often from 2013, when it began to install minor updates itself. The rest stay open, and an open site can be defaced: only that site changes, its customer writes in, and it sends spam from the host's mail address until it is cleaned up. A medium or high skill customer sometimes cleans it up within three days; otherwise the player does it from Monitoring or from the ticket in half an hour. A customer whose site is defaced three times in a year cancels. The Monitoring tab lists the customers' apps next to the company's own software, with chips for Own software and Customer sites. Notify customers writes to everyone running an app with a given vulnerability, at half an hour per ten customers; about half of those who would not update by themselves answer with a ticket asking for help. The player answers with a one-off update, managed updates, or does it for free. Most customers who asked buy the one-off, some take managed updates at a monthly fee that raises their price, and some refuse; customers with a basic budget never take managed updates. Doing it for free takes 20 minutes and makes the customer happier. A managed site is updated within a day of every patch without further work.

**Software versions and vulnerabilities.** Every installed software has a version, the year of the release it runs, and a new install runs this year's release. Every software family ships a release each year, and each release has a few vulnerabilities of its own: hosting panels, hypervisors and mail servers (economy/security-advisories.md, Security advisories, Generated vulnerabilities). A vulnerability is open from the day its patch comes out until the software is updated. A low or high one raises a banner when monitoring is installed, and a critical one stops the game with a sheet that opens the Security app. Open vulnerabilities cost nothing until an attacker finds them: each day every software that has customers on it risks a compromise, and the risk grows the longer a vulnerability stays open. A compromised panel host or hypervisor behaves like a compromised node from an advisory: its sites or virtual machines are down until clean-up, its customers get a data leak notice, send abuse reports and write bad reviews, and the host sends spam. A compromised mail host sends spam until it is cleaned up.

**Monitoring tab.** Nagiosaurus is a Pear Store app that needs the Monitoring skill. Owning it adds a Monitoring tab to the Security app that lists every installed software with its version, its worst open vulnerability, the days it has been exposed, the server it runs on and whether it is compromised, the most exposed first.

**Updating software.** Update on the software tile, in its right-click menu and in the Monitoring tab installs this year's release and closes every vulnerability patched so far. The customers on the software are down for a short time while it updates (economy/security-advisories.md, Security advisories, Updating software). The update can be queued for a technician at the server: panel and mail updates are panel work, hypervisor updates are server work. The technician rule Update vulnerable software, set in the Staff app next to the repair rules and off by default, queues an update for every software with an open high or critical vulnerability.

**Breaches and ransom.** A critical vulnerability left open on the player's own software for more than a month can end in a breach instead of a plain compromise (economy/security-advisories.md, Security advisories, Breaches and ransom). The attackers steal the customer data on the host and an extortion ticket arrives from them, as an alert that opens the ticket in Support. It names the sum and says the data is for sale online. Pay ransom books the money; one time in ten the data is sold anyway. Refuse, Ignore, and a missed 72-hour deadline all sell it. A sale costs every customer on the host 40 happiness, makes 30% of them cancel and 20% write a 1-star review, lowers awareness in every profile by 20%, brings a press banner and, after 30 days, perhaps a data protection fine (a small chance before 25 May 2018, a coin flip under the GDPR). The Security app keeps a Breaches card with the date, host, ransom, decision and outcome of every case.

**Hiring.** The Staff app lists candidates on the job market with their name, role, skill and salary ask for 40 hours a week and per hour. Job ads cost money, and better ads bring more candidates. Hiring asks for the team first and shows the monthly cost at that team's hours with the runway before and after the hire; the first hire starts in the team with the fewest hours, the cheapest way in. Letting someone go asks first and shows the monthly saving; they leave the same day and are paid for the days worked that month, with no notice period.

**Teams and shifts.** Each employee belongs to a team. The Shifts view in the Staff app lists the teams, sets each team's start hour and length (1 to 12 hours) for every weekday, and shows how many people are on shift in each hour of the week, so several teams can cover the clock. A new game has an Office team (weekdays 9 to 17) and a Part-time team (weekdays 9 to 11). Salary is paid for the hours actually worked, and the shift is the employee's whole capacity: they work actions in priority order until the day's minutes run out and the rest waits for the next workday. Bots have no hours; they work around the clock at their fixed rate.

**Activity log.** Every action an employee, a bot, the automation or the player takes on a customer's tickets, invoices or services goes into an activity log with who did it and how many minutes it took. A service's log in the Customers app shows its own entries and the customer-wide ones (invoices, suspensions). The log keeps 90 days, at most 3,000 entries.

**Staff app.** The Team view lists each employee with role, skill, salary, assignment and a bar per task type that fills as training finishes. Selecting an employee opens their training plan, where you pick task types as cards, set the assignment (work queue, trainer or forums), answer a raise request or let them go. The Job market view shows candidates as cards grouped by role with a Hire button, and posts standard or premium job ads. The roles in play are support agents, bookkeepers, accountants, marketing managers and technicians. A technician's page also sets their location, lists the operations queued there with a Cancel for each, and holds the repair rules. Bots appear in the Team view after the employees with a Bot badge, their tasks and their subscription price; selecting one shows what it handles and cancels the subscription.

**AI bots.** Bots unlock late through research. They work fast and cost a monthly subscription instead of a salary. They never gain skill, handle only routine task types, and sometimes make mistakes that create follow-up work, such as a wrong ticket answer that angers a customer.

The Automation skill (tier 3, after Employee management and Monitoring) puts "Clipsy Reminders" in the Pear Store, its own subscription with the yearly Pear price rise, and lets a hosted desk switch on its generative AI from 2023. Clipsy sends payment reminders: every reminder that is due, whatever the billing rules say, and it takes what the automation leaves. The AI agent of a hosted desk (Zendusk AI agent, Freddie AI Agent) answers the desk's how-to and billing tickets; it is switched on in the desk's AI page for a monthly add-on and listed in the Staff app as a bot. Before 2023 no bot answers tickets, so a company without staff answers every ticket itself. Bots work around the clock at twice the base work rate and never learn. The AI agent picks the right reply 60% of the time on how-to tickets and half the time on billing tickets, below any trained human; a wrong reply brings the usual follow-up in the same thread, and the agent tries again. It bills every reply in tokens, and since each reply rereads the whole thread, a retry costs more than the first answer. After four replies on one ticket it leaves the ticket to staff and the player, and the customer resents the runaround. A solved ticket costs about a third of what a support agent costs for one, so the agent saves money without being free: many customers, an outage or a price rise make for a large monthly bill. Reply drafting puts a drafted reply above the composer of each how-to and billing ticket, right with the same odds, and bills each draft in tokens. The Staff app shows the month's replies, drafts, tokens, cost and cost per solved ticket, and the bill arrives as one ledger line on the first of the month. During office hours the staff take the queue first and the bots take what is left. A reminder from Clipsy now and then confuses the customer into a billing ticket, and once in a long while a bot does something memorable, such as telling a customer to speed up their website by deleting the disk. The customer then loses happiness and opens an angry billing ticket; the bot never touches data. Only one copy of each bot exists. ChadGPT Support, the earlier ticket bot of the Pear Store, is gone; saves that had it load without it.
