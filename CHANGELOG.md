# Changelog

Newest first. One `## YYYY-MM-DD` heading per day, one `- ` bullet per change. A bullet is one player-facing sentence that says what changed in the game, with no file names, code names or em dashes. The game reads this file at build time and shows the bullets a newer build has when it offers a reload, so every change that lands in main adds its bullet here.

## 2026-10-29

- The Security app lists every compromised host, including virtual machine clusters, game panels and the mail host, with a Clean up button that works without Monitoring.
- The Security app now sits in the dock from the start, so you can clean up a compromised host without waiting for an alert.
- Loans moved from the Bank app into a new Loans page in Finance, and loan alerts open it directly.
- Marketing basics now opens the Reviews and Community sections in Marketing, and new reviews only send you a notification once you can read them there.
- Event organisers only ask you for sponsoring once you have learned Marketing basics.
- A VM or game server stuck on a switched-off server whose cluster's control plane is down can be recovered to a server of another cluster in the same location by remote hands, by you or by a friend.
- Restoring a defaced customer site now needs a backup of it, and without one you can only tell the customer to rebuild it from their own copy, which costs happiness.
- The sheet that opens after a training starts with a Now available page that checks off everything the skill unlocks and what you can do with it.
- The Used tab has listings from the first day: servers and network gear sold since before 2005 turn up on eBuy, WebHostingTalc and Telegrump right away, one to three years old.
- Every server has a screen now: a Console tile on managed servers and the controller's page open it in its own window, a KVM opens it from its page, and clicking the blue console end on the back of a server in the rack plugs in a monitor on the spot.
- The RAID level moves from the server page into the BIOS setup on that screen, entered with Del during the boot splash or at the firmware prompt, and a server left in the setup stays down until you leave it.
- Your Email or Support icon in the dock now shows how many tickets and requests are waiting from the start, and the Dock badges upgrade adds counts to the other apps.
- The Backbone menu has Report an issue, which opens a new GitHub issue with your game build filled in.
- The Marketing app opens on Channels once you learn Marketing campaigns, its Domain page has proper margins, and the skill sheet lists the six sections the skill adds.
- The Backbone menu links to the new Discord server, where you can chat, share screenshots, request features and report bugs.
- The browser tab shows the new Backbone logo.
- A customer whose services all kept their SLA uptime through a month gains a little happiness at the billing date, so steady service slowly builds loyalty.
- Replies, order steps and other hand work now land at the minute they are done instead of at the next full hour, a server boots in three minutes and a new domain shows as registering for two.
- A custom order now sends the customer three emails, the quote, the confirmation and the notice that everything is live, instead of one per step.
- A support desk without reply templates still offers the reply that settles the ticket, so a lost panel login or a price complaint can be answered.
- While you use free Notmail, only half of the businesses that would order by email write to you; private users, gamers and developers do not mind.
- You buy your company domain in the Marketing app under Domain, which is now installed for free from the start; the rest of the app opens with the Marketing campaigns skill.
- Server manufacturers differ in more than price: SuperMacro releases models four months early and sells them a year longer at 5% more power, Hewlett-Packet releases three months late, stops selling a year earlier and draws 5% less, and Lenovoid releases six months late.
- The Shop has a Sort menu with Price low to high, Price high to low, Newest and Lowest power, and each card shows its release month.
- A racked server outside a cluster can be burned in, quick for 6 hours or full for 48: it shows which used parts are worn, a full test also fails weak parts and a passed one lowers the failure rate for a year, and used parts bought on the board or eBuy refund half the price when one fails within 14 days.
- A new company starts with a free Notmail address that takes order emails without a domain, and moving to mail under your own domain is an optional upgrade.
- The Email app has a Settings button that opens the email settings.
- Getting started now begins with your first server, and the domain and email steps come later.
- On the desk, the Terminal shows your desktop notes and Getting started in ~/Desktop, and the new edit command opens any file in a simple editor that saves with Ctrl+S.
- A power outage that takes servers dark now cuts them off uncleanly, and they boot again once the power is back.
- Datacenter power feeds can fail on their own: servers with two power supplies on a dual-fed rack ride through it with a notification, the rest go dark.
- Loading a saved game no longer opens the Pear OS update sheet; What's new in the logo menu shows the full changelog in a larger sheet, grouped by version.
- The speed buttons show one to three arrows for 1×, 2× and 4×, and a new skip button runs the clock at 12 game hours a second until something happens.
- The Email app no longer installs a mail server with one click; its self-hosted view shows the mail server options and an Open button to Locations, where you install one from a server's Add Software tile.
- The menu bar shows where you are with the local weather, and you can drive to any of your datacenters for a fare while the clock skips ahead and the computer stays locked.
- Hand work at the datacenter you stand in starts at once; anywhere else a popover shows the remote hands price to accept or decline, next to sending a friend or a technician.
- Getting started has a step to install the Network app from the Pear Store, right before you order your domain.
- The Terminal is now a small shell: list, create, read, move and remove files and folders on your desk and on every server you log into, and chain commands with && and ;.
- Running apt update and apt upgrade on a server installs its outstanding software and PHP updates, after asking you to confirm the downtime.
- You can drag a device to another position in its rack once its power cords are unplugged, and a plugged-in device stays put.
- A new game opens the Getting started guide right away instead of a welcome popup, and the guide now starts with naming your company, ordering your domain and booking email, each with an Open button to the app where you do it.
- The website step in Getting started asks you to build your website and list a product on its Admin page.
- Clubs and meetups now write every month or two and ask for free servers for their event, and keeping them up earns know-how, awareness, leads and sometimes a recommended hire.
- The Marketing app has a Community section that lists running and past sponsorships with their outcome; a sponsored service that is down for more than two hours costs you two bad reviews instead.
- Servers now come with Indel or AMB CPUs, and from 2020 the ARM line with Ampear CPUs, each with its own cores, speed per core, power draw and price, and the lead between them changes over the years.
- Every socket of a server holds the same CPU, a CPU change replaces all sockets in one job, VMs move live only between servers of one CPU vendor unless a cluster runs Compatibility mode, and some VPS, game server and cloud orders need x86.
- The Shop sells KVMs, console servers and an OOB switch, which let you power servers on from afar, open any console from the Browser or with `console` in the Terminal, and recover gear when the network is down.
- A server can now stop at a BIOS prompt after a part swap or a hard power cut, and switches can hang during a firmware update or lock you out after a port group change, until someone continues or recovers them.
- Management devices reachable from the internet show up in the Security app with their firmware's vulnerabilities.
- Weekly challenge seeds start from the title screen, a run can be exported and replayed, and a leaderboard shows verified runs.
- Loading a save shows a Pear OS update sheet with what changed since you last played, with an Open button for each app it names, and the logo menu has a What's new item that lists the whole changelog.
- You can book extra power on a rented rack share in steps of 0.25 kW up to twice its base limit, billed monthly with the rent and active after three days.
- A server's power, front panel, Change parts and a new Terminal action that connects over ssh now sit together on its Actions page, and its ports and bond have their own Ports page.
- New servers start without a bond and use only their first cabled port until you set Failover or Load balance.
- New customers write throughout the day, offices during business hours and private customers mostly in the evening, instead of all at once in the morning mail.
- The shop filter now appears on every shop page and fits on one line with dropdowns for form factor, cores, RAM and disk.

- Servers have a power button you can press to boot or shut down cleanly or hold to force off, a reset button, and Unplug power pulls every cord, which can corrupt data on a running server.

- Clicking a power inlet on the back of a server plugs its cord in or pulls it, and a powered-off device can be dragged on the rack front to a free spot.

- A notice about a site that is not on your network offers only Reject, and the reply tells the sender it is not yours and how to find the right host with whois.
- You can plan a maintenance window on a server, a cluster or a location, and the customers it affects get a notice email; downtime inside a window announced a day ahead costs no happiness, tickets or SLA credit.

- The clock in the menu bar counts minutes in steps of five between the hourly ticks.
