# Backbone masterplan

This is the work plan for the next stretch of Backbone. It lists what is built, what is in flight and the lanes still to do, in order, with the full brief for each. Read CLAUDE.md, docs/design/gdd.md, docs/design/economy.md and DESIGN.md before starting a lane.

## Working rules

- The owner wants the game complete and playable and authorizes autonomous decisions. Record every decision in docs/design/gdd.md (rules) and docs/design/economy.md (numbers, with real-world anchors).
- Design preferences from past corrections: no dropdowns (segmented controls, tabs, cards, chips); card grids and sidebars like the Shop and Billing apps; right-click menus for per-object actions; UI copy is labels and values only, captions are full sentences with a verb, never leak internals or name customer types on individual customers; every real company is a pun (Dwell, Hewlett-Packet, Sysko, Goggle, Hetzna); dry humour; no em dashes anywhere.
- Player actions live in src/sim/90-actions and the UI calls them through the store's `act`. Sim-produced text goes through src/i18n/en.ts keys; every event key has `.title` and `.text` (tests/event-keys.test.ts checks it). UI labels stay inline.
- Every sim change has a seeded test. Old saves must load with sane defaults; the game is in development, so no compensation or grandfathering work.
- Hotspot files (src/sim/50-state.ts, src/sim/88-tick/tick.ts, src/sim/91-save/save.ts, src/i18n/en.ts, src/ui/apps/registry.ts, src/ui/store.ts): keep edits additive and put logic in the lane's own modules. Window sizes live in WINDOW_SIZES in src/ui/store.ts.
- Commits: message of at most 4 words, no description, no co-author lines, never mention an AI tool. Commit and push each green piece; main deploys to GitHub Pages.
- Verify each lane with `npm run typecheck`, `npm test` (redirect output to a file and search it for FAIL), `npm run build`, `npm run balance` (report steady and solo medians before and after, keep them within about half a year, no bankruptcies) and, for early-game changes, `npx tsx scripts/happiness.ts` for 2005. Check the UI with Playwright in light and dark; to change state in the page, import the store through its exact module URL from performance entries. Save screenshots to .impeccable/review/<lane>-*.png.
- The owner plays on the Vite dev server (port 5173) from this checkout, so merging code here reloads their page; windows and the Support selection survive reloads (src/ui/session.ts).

Balance today (main 3ba75ac): steady medians L2 2006.0, L3 2006.8, L4 2008.6, L5 2011.9, L6 2017.9; solo L5 2012.8; 0 of 20 bankrupt in both. 529 tests pass.

Design fully, record in gdd.md (a new "Addresses and internet" section) and numbers with real anchors in economy.md, then build:
1. Addresses. Every VM and server has a private address always (predefined per location range, shown, not managed). Public IPv4: every server gets one by default where its location provides it (home line: the line's one address, dynamic or static; colo and datacenter: from the provider, priced per address per month, rising over the years with IPv4 exhaustion: anchor RIPE's last /8 in 2012 and run-out in 2019, market prices through the 2020s). A VM that must be reachable from the internet needs a public address assigned (IPv4 or IPv6). Later the player can become a LIR with their own blocks (one-time and yearly fees, a block size, real anchors) and announce them. IPv6 unlocks later (anchor World IPv6 Launch 2012 or earlier availability at providers), almost free per address, assigned per server or VM. Show addresses on the server and VM pages and a simple address list per location (labels and values). Actions through 90-actions: assign, release, buy more.
2. Customer requirements. Some customers need a dedicated IPv4 (SSL before SNI, mail senders, game servers), and from some year a growing share requires IPv6. Orders state it in their spec (specText) and fail or lose the sale without a free address. Dedicated IPs are a priced resource in Pricing (per address), like the other rates.
3. Reputation. Each public address (or block) has a mail reputation. Spam from a compromised customer or a spamming customer lowers it; low reputation brings tickets from customers whose mail bounces, then blocklisting of the address; it recovers slowly after the cause is gone, faster with action (delist request as a hand task). Dynamic home addresses start with poor mail reputation (they sit on dynamic-range blocklists): mail hosting from a dynamic line works badly. The web hosting lane after this one will make compromised sites send spam into this; expose a small sim function that adds spam volume to an address.
4. Internet always costs money. Every home line has a monthly price (fix DSL 16000; anchor 2005 German prices). Dynamic lines get a forced reconnect every night around 3:00 (anchor the German 24-hour disconnect): a short outage each night and a new address. Business and large customers notice and refuse to buy or churn on a dynamic line (use the customer profile's budget/segment); private customers and small shops do not notice. A static line (SDSL) avoids it. Show the line's address type and the nightly reconnect where the line is shown.
5. The start. A new game offers no product lines: everything is disabled until the player can deliver it. Lines unlock as capability appears (web hosting once a panel runs on a server with a public address, VPS once a hypervisor runs, dedicated once a spare server can be handed over, and so on: pick the rules, record them). The friends still wait for web hosting.
6. Tutorial. The existing Getting started document on the desktop becomes a short step tutorial: buy your first server, mount and power it, add internet (pick a line), install software, enable your first product, take your first customer. Each step is checked off by the sim (Checklist), labels and full sentences, no filler copy, no em dashes. Keep it small.
7. Balance model (src/sim/balance/model.ts) and scripts/happiness.ts follow: paid internet, address costs, dynamic-line limits on early customers. Report steady and solo medians before and after; keep within about half a year. No bankruptcies.
8. Old saves load: servers and VMs get addresses filled in; offered product lines stay offered; home line prices apply from the next month. In-dev: no compensation work.
9. Seeded tests: every server gets an IPv4; a public VM needs an assigned address; out of addresses blocks the sale; IPv6 requirement; dynamic reconnect outage at night and business customers refusing; reputation drops with spam and recovers; new game offers no lines and lines unlock with capability; old saves load.

Verify typecheck, tests, build, balance, happiness harness, and a Playwright run of a new game through the tutorial to the friends active, plus the address list and a server's addresses, light and dark. Commit at green checkpoints and record decisions in the design docs.

Added by the owner while this lane ran (part of this lane): "private customers take waaay longer to notice outages like 10-20 days even, randomize it some, a person may notice it waaay faster, gamers usually notice it during their gaming hours instantly but not at night so when doing maintenance on gameservers it can be easily done, also gaming happens more on the weekends aka when there is no workday the next day, and during work hours and at the early morning hours nobody is using them". Build a usage-by-hour model per segment and product and make noticing an outage depend on it: private customers notice slowly with a long random tail (most take days, some 10 to 20 days, a few within hours); game servers follow an hour and weekday curve (evenings high, peak Friday and Saturday evenings and nights before a free day, low in work hours, near zero 02:00 to 08:00), so outages in active hours are noticed at once and short ones in dead hours often not at all; businesses notice fast in business hours and less at night. Feed this into the outage penalty, outage tickets (src/sim/60-infra/network.ts OUTAGE_WRITE_CHANCE, incidents.ts refunds) and churn: an unnoticed outage costs little or nothing. Seeded tests: game server outage at 04:00 Tuesday unnoticed when short; Saturday 21:00 noticed at once; private delay distribution.

Owner additions (translated from German), also sent to the running lane: the home line can be upgraded over the years so the basement stays useful long term, for example as the off-site backup location. A business line for the home is static, comes with several static IPs and can buy more; business lines are relatively expensive. A dynamic line only allows web hosting behind its one dynamic IP, and the 3:00 reconnect means high-critical customers who notice at once can still be taken on but end up unhappy (penalty and churn) rather than refused. Datacenter IPs are cheaper than business-line IPs: about EUR 1 per IP per month at the start, falling with volume as the company grows (record the tiers); later the player gets their own networks through their own AS. Every IP has a reputation that spam, abuse content and phishing sites on it lower (use the notice and abuse tickets in src/sim/40-support/notices.ts and security.ts).

## Smaller gaps

- Email orders charge no setup fee; the manual quote flow has no place to set one.
- Setup fees show under payments in Finance rather than in their own category.
- Ballooning is not a separate hypervisor trait; a custom VM without a panel cannot be resized.
- Customers have no credit or suspend action, tickets have no staff assignment, and cancelling a bot subscription has no confirmation.
- No delivery wait and no cheap starter tower in the Shop.
- The clock starts paused after a reload, and apps other than Support forget their selection.
- A full hand playtest of year one has not been done.
